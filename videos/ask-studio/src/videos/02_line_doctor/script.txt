[hook] She painted a line down the middle of a highway, by hand, because nobody else would.
[town] June McCarroll was a doctor in a tiny desert town in California.
[truck] One day, driving out to a patient, a truck came roaring straight down the middle of the road.
[sand] She swerved into the sand. And she got an idea.
[idea] A white line. Right down the middle. Your side. My side.
[county] She took it to the county. "Paint? On a road? Waste of money."
[door] She asked again. Ignored. And again. Ignored.
[store] So she bought a can of white paint, [brush] and painted the line herself.
[stare] Drivers slowed down to stare. "That's the crazy lady doctor."
[stretch] But on her stretch of road, everyone stayed on their own side.
[club] Then the women of her town joined her. [map] Then the women's clubs of the whole state. Letters. Petitions. Thousands of names.
[years] Seven years later, California said yes, [miles] and painted thousands of miles of white line.
[modern] You see, she didn't pass a law. She just drew one. And every driver obeys it.
[end] Next time you drive, look down. That line started with a doctor and a paintbrush.

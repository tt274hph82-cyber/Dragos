[hook] For forty years, the stop sign wasn't red. And that wasn't a mistake.
[detroit] Detroit, 1915. Cars are new, crossings are chaos, and nobody stops for anybody.
[square] So the city nails up a little white square with black letters. STOP.
[night] Problem? At night, nobody could see it. [wall] And it looked like every other sign.
[room] In 1923, a group of highway engineers had a strange idea: the more sides a sign has, the more dangerous the spot.
[board] A circle for railroad crossings. Eight sides for stop. [snow] So you'd know it from behind, even covered in snow.
[paint] Then they picked a colour. Not red. Yellow.
[fade] Why? Because red paint faded. A few summers in the sun, and red turned pink, then grey.
[montage] So for thirty years, America stopped at yellow.
[works] Then in 1954, paint finally got tough enough to stay red. And red got its job.
[today] Next time you hit the brakes, look at that sign. It took forty years to look that simple.

Facts and numbers I assumed

  • It began in July 1518 in Strasbourg with a woman recorded as Frau Troffea, who danced in the street for days.
  • About 30 or more people had joined within a week (some sources say 34).
  • "Some accounts say 400" by late summer: a chroniclers' figure, not a firm count, so the narration hedges it.
  • Physicians blamed "hot blood" and prescribed more dancing; the council opened guild halls, built a stage and hired musicians.
  • Reports of many deaths are disputed, so the film only shows dancers collapsing.
  • The dancers were taken to a shrine of St. Vitus in the hills near Saverne, and the outbreak faded by September. The cause is still debated.

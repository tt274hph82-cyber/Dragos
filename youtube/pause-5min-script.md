# What If You Could Pause Time? (full episode, about 5 minutes)

**Hero:** Juno ("you"). **Cast:** Mo (best friend), Sis (little sister), Mum, The Suit.
**Rule of the power:** time pauses only while you hold your breath. Breathe, and it plays again.
**The 24-second cut we already made** becomes the cold open, and also works as a YouTube Short teaser.

Approximate timings assume about 2.6 spoken words a second.

---

## 1. Cold open: three quick demos (0:00–0:24)
You drop your phone. *Pause.* Catch it. *Play.*
A paper ball flies at your face. *Pause.* Step aside. *Play.*
Your best friend, mid-laugh. *Pause.* Not even blinking.

**TITLE:** What if you could pause time, but only while you hold your breath?

## 2. The premise and its rules (0:24–1:05)
It starts on a Tuesday. You hiccup in the middle of maths, you hold your breath to make it stop, and the room goes silent.
The clock's second hand is stuck. A pencil hangs in mid-air. Your teacher's mouth is frozen open, halfway through a word.
You gasp. And everything starts again, like nothing happened. Nobody else noticed. Not even Mo.
So you test it. Three rules.
One: hold your breath, and the world stops. Breathe, and it plays.
Two: anything you're touching moves with you. Everything else is stuck.
Three: you can only stop it for as long as you can hold your breath. And right now, that's forty seconds.

## 3. The fun part (1:05–2:05)
So you train. Every night, in the bath, you count. Forty seconds. Fifty. A minute.
And life gets very, very easy.
Late for the bus? *Pause.* Walk to the front of the queue. *Play.*
Last slice of pizza? *Pause.* It's yours. Mo is still reaching for the box.
Sports day. The whistle goes. *Pause.* You stroll to the finish line and stand there, out of breath, as the whole school freezes mid-sprint.
Mum asks who ate the cookies. *Pause.* You brush the crumbs onto the cat. *Play.*
A wasp lands on your sandwich. *Pause.* You carry the whole sandwich outside, wasp and all.
You hide Mo's shoes on the roof. You put the cat on the fridge. You draw a moustache on your own reflection, just because you can.
For two weeks, you're the luckiest person alive.

## 4. The not-so-fun part (2:05–3:15)
But you start to notice things.
Your head pounds after every pause. Your chest burns. Each time, the burning comes a little sooner. At night you lie awake, counting. Not sheep. Seconds.
Here's why. The urge to breathe isn't your body running out of oxygen. It's carbon dioxide building up in your blood. Your brain reads it as an alarm, and the alarm only gets louder. That's real.
So you try a trick from the internet: breathe fast and deep before you hold it. It works. You last much longer.
It's also the trick that makes swimmers black out underwater without any warning. Breathing fast flushes out the carbon dioxide, so the alarm comes late, sometimes after your oxygen has already run low. That's real too.
And there's one more problem nobody warned you about. When the world is paused, you are not.
A minute here, a minute there. Pause a hundred times a day, and you've lived a hundred extra minutes the world never saw. That's twenty-five extra days a year. You're getting older faster than everyone you know.

## 5. Someone powerful notices (3:15–3:55)
Then a man in a grey suit starts appearing.
Outside the school gates. Across the street from your house. In the back of the café, watching you.
One day he sits down next to you on a bench and puts a phone on the table. Security footage from sports day. In one frame you're at the starting line. In the next, you're at the finish. Zero frames in between.
He knows your name. He knows your school. He knows exactly how long you can hold your breath.
"Interesting," he says. "Can you do it again?"
You hold your breath. When the world freezes, you run.

## 6. The choice (3:55–4:35)
You make it home. Your little sister is in the garden, by the pond, chasing a ball. She's four. She can't swim yet.
She slips. She goes under.
You haven't breathed properly since the bench. Your lungs are already burning.
If you pause now, you might black out. If you don't, you might be too late.
You hold your breath one more time.
The world stops. The water stops. Your sister stops, halfway under.
You pull her out. You're counting. Forty. Fifty. Your vision goes grey at the edges.
You breathe.
She coughs, and she's fine. She's fine.
Behind the garden fence, something clicks. A camera.

## 7. Cliffhanger (4:35–5:00)
The next morning, there's a letter under the door. No stamp. No name. Just one line:
"How long can you hold your breath, Juno? We'd like to find out."
Part one of two.
So how long can you hold yours? Tell us in the comments. And don't try it underwater.
